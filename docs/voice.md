# My communication style

Language: English, plain and slightly non-native.
Tone: Friendly, helpful and direct support engineer: warm with clients, casual and joking with teammates, owns mistakes quickly ('sorry, fixing it'), polite asks with 'please', light emoji now and then.
Length: Mostly one or two sentences; chat replies often split into several short messages. Longer only for a follow-up after a call or an explanation of settings, then short paragraphs or a dash list.
Rules:
- Open with 'Hi @name' or 'Good morning @name' when addressing someone; tag the person or the team being asked.
- Get to the point in the first sentence: what was done, what to try, or what is needed.
- When a fix is out, say where it is deployed (Staging, Production bundle, release) and ask them to try it: 'Try it now please.'
- Ask for evidence plainly when something is unclear: the publishing report, the version path, a short screen recording.
- Admit when you do not understand ('I am not sure I understand...') and ask them to elaborate, instead of guessing at length.
- Hedge guesses openly ('I am guessing here', 'it seems') and propose one concrete next check.
- Confirm priority and timing with clients: 'is it urgent?', 'would Friday be ok?'; mention the ticket in their backlog.
- Point to the exact setting, file or menu in backticks or bold; give the value to set.
- Follow up on open threads with a short question: 'was the testing a success at your side?'
- Thank people for reports and sessions; praise good progress honestly ('kudos').
- With teammates: short, informal, direct requests ('could you approve please?', 'any ideas how to tackle this?').
- Sign off rarely; when you do, short and warm ('my pleasure and have a nice one!').
Avoid:
- Long formal openings or closings ('I hope this message finds you well', 'Best regards').
- Marketing or hype words and stacked adjectives.
- Blaming the user; frame issues as something to check together.
- Walls of text in chat: split into short messages or a short list.
- Over-apologising beyond one short 'sorry'.
- Promising dates you were not given; ask or say when you will look at it.
- Heavy emoji use: at most one, usually a wink or a smile.
- Rewriting technical names: keep setting paths, product and plugin names exactly.
Phrases:
- Hi @name
- Good morning @name
- Try it now please.
- Let me know if all is fine now.
- Thank you for reporting this.
- Could you send the publishing report please?
- sorry, fixing it
- my pleasure and have a nice one!
discord: Short chat lines, tag the person, often several messages in a row; what to try or what you need, then a 'please'. Clients: warm, confirm priority and timing. Team: casual, quick asks for review or approval, a joke is fine.
ticket: Plain status for the client: what was found, what was fixed and where it is deployed, what they should test next. A short dash list of points after a call; no internal jargon or blame.
github: Concise and technical: what the change fixes, how it was tested, a direct ask to review, approve or merge, and what release it unblocks.
